# Defect register

## ML-S1 - Whole-PC freeze shortly after Windows launch (2026-11-07)

**Reported symptom:** The game appeared, then the entire Windows PC froze or restarted within seconds. Earlier short frame-time checks did not test standalone exported GPU startup or sustained memory use.

**Evidence and scope:** Windows recorded an unclean restart with no bug-check code; scoped searches found no current crash dump, Godot application-crash event or resource-exhaustion event. This does not identify the cause or exclude a driver-side failure. Static review found finite loops and retained cache lifetimes, but the application had no explicit frame limit and could draw around 200 FPS on the earlier test machine. A 60 FPS application cap reduces unnecessary rendering while preserving VSync and interpolated animation. This is a load mitigation, not a confirmed repair for the system freeze.

**Whole-class regression:** The runtime stability suite checks the effective frame limit on engine startup and repeatedly exercises full scene destruction, menu replacement, UI scaling, new-colony resets and dimension-dependent terrain replacement. It checks retired node identities, warmed node/object counts and exact cache pixel budgets. Its optional timed headless soak uses the normal production frame loop and reports tracked memory/counts with bounded-growth assertions. An external watchdog bounds duration and process private memory. Headless checks and software WebGL capture do not establish native GPU-driver stability. Evidence and remaining limits are recorded in work 4.

## ML-V2 - Object proportions and flat build controls do not scale (2026-11-07)

**Reported symptom:** Furniture and machinery looked implausibly tiny or oversized, and permanently numbered buttons for every object would not scale with the catalog.

**Repair and regression scope:** Buildings now reserve their complete grid footprint, with shared bottom-left anchors for placement, inspection, previews, cancellation, construction and foundation protection. Beds span two tiles, machinery spans two by two, and the beacon spans two by three. Renderer tests check complete preview geometry at multiple zooms, sprite fit and substantial furniture dimensions; simulation tests cover all occupied cells and both support columns, overlap, cancellation/refund and invalidation during assignment, work and completion. Build controls are organized into categories with focused item lists; UI and real-input acceptance cover navigation and catalog reachability. Rendered acceptance caught unbounded menu/detail content: at 720x560 the build scroll viewport extended below the window, and at 150% scale a long selected-item description enlarged the cave beyond the screen before Cancel shrank it, moving the target under a stationary pointer. Layout bounds and stable desktop world coordinates are now explicit regression criteria. Native testing also found that routing repeatedly used the general footprint lookup merely to find 1x1 ladder segments, producing 3x simulation spikes. A direct ladder kind/anchor query preserves routes without cached state; a same-state profile and ladder lifecycle tests verify the repair, and native timing is rechecked on the final build. Final acceptance is recorded in work 3.

## ML-A2 — Digging moved the tool without a readable strike

**Reported symptom:** Motion is smooth, but settlers appear to hold the hammer and bob it slightly without swinging or impact.

**Root cause and repair:** The previous physical implement used fixed shaft/head offsets from a hand driven by a small sine wave. The new presentation-only rig has two hands gripping a rotating pick, a raised wind-up, accelerated downswing, contact hold, body lean, recoil and recovery. The tip lands slightly inside the active job's exposed rock face in every cardinal direction. A bounded flash, fracture and six chips originate at that point; no simulation timing or resource rule changes. The per-job clock freezes with pause and is suppressed during travel, rest, death, cancellation and reduced motion. A live-job guard removes a canceled pick while paused, before the next simulation tick.

**Whole-class check and proof:** The 128-check digging suite samples production geometry and actual CaveArt drawing calls, including both hands, wide shaft rotation, contact location, bounded/timed debris, cycle continuity and 60/120Hz at 1x/3x. The retained 8f3655a artist fails twelve behavioral checks: zero shaft rotation, only 0.418 tile tip excursion, no aimed contact and no debris. An arrival counterexample caught a zero-movement render frame that started a strike before interpolation reached the job; requiring rendered endpoint arrival fixes it. Independent review then found a ceiling stroke starting inside rock and horizontal strokes briefly retreating before contact. A 24-check gate across every direction and both facings fails ten checks on that exact draft. The repair uses a low wind-up for ceiling work and guides the pick tip inward along a curve, solving the grips along the rigid shaft. All 24 pass with the preexisting motion bounds unchanged; all five Godot suites pass 835 checks. These drawing/lifecycle tests do not certify pixels or native GPU pacing; rendered acceptance, review and delivery remain in the imported motion record and current migration work.

## ML-A1 — Frame rate did not guarantee smooth colony animation

**Reported symptom:** The Windows build's animation is not smooth; the owner requires at least 60fps and smooth visible movement.

**Investigation and root cause:** The renderer reads colonist positions directly from the 10Hz authoritative simulation. Even a renderer drawing above 60fps therefore holds each position for several frames and jumps on the next simulation tick. Gait used a separate wall-clock sine rather than traveled distance, and the resting flag could draw settlers lying down while they were still walking to beds. Earlier 52–57fps samples also fall below this request's performance target.

**Repair and whole-class gate:** Presentation interpolates between unchanged simulation samples; visible distance drives gait, and work/climb weights blend every frame. Fractional time survives menus, pause and focus loss; rest poses wait for visible arrival. The 291-check motion suite covers 1x/3x and 60/120Hz, directions, turns, work/rest, reduced motion and actual main-scene lifecycle methods. The original direct-position adapter fails all 36 cadence checks; the old resume reset/rebase path fails 108 of 168 targeted checks. Controlled native legacy movement repeats 95.8% of moving frames at 1x despite 236fps rendering; interpolation has no repeated moving frames in the comparable native workload.

**Excavation hitch:** Native measurement also found four 63–68ms frames exactly coinciding with terrain-hash changes. Retaining per-cell vector commands alone still rerasterized the full cave and left 23–46ms stalls. The final repair shares those commands across dirty 4x4-cell render regions at the original resolution, preserving rock/growth order, filtering guards and a common fractional origin. Sixty-two tests inspect production draw signals, dependencies, region scheduling, reset behavior and seam geometry: an interior dig regenerates five cells and four small regions; camera changes regenerate none. The original painter fails four CPU-locality assertions, and the full-target raster approach fails four GPU-locality assertions. Same-clock reset and independent Control-snapping counterexamples were fixed and rechecked. On the final RTX 4090 native workload, topology frames are 10.495/15.067/12.763/10.046ms; all measured active intervals stay below 16.7ms. Timing uses render-completion observation and ordinary engine-input replay in the matching native engine; OS-input/compositor certification remains unavailable. Exact hashes, startup/capture exclusions, visual parity and delivery are recorded in the imported motion record and current migration work acceptance.

## ML-V1 — Flat terrain and blockout graphics missed the living-cave brief

**Reported symptom:** "I still want good graphics" and "Also why is the cave so flat". The owner selected detailed stylized illustration.

**Investigation and root cause:** The first Mosslight map placed all nine starter buildings on a single horizontal floor, while the renderer outlined every rock cell and drew small geometric sprites. Successful survival tests and responsive controls established functionality but did not establish the requested visual quality or vertical colony gameplay.

**Repair and whole-class gate:** Work 7 replaces the flat colony with an elevated roost, four occupied elevations and a lower quarry reached by extending a real ladder. The focused terrain gate fails seven of thirteen checks on the old source and passes all thirteen on the new map; the complete simulation passes 277 checks. An ordinary keyboard/mouse expedition physically descends the shaft, collects ore and roots, builds a beacon and wins at 1200 simulation seconds with all four settlers at full health. Independent full-resolution visual review accepts the illustrated backdrop, painted stone, surface vegetation, distinct buildings and readable settlers at 1280x800, 720x560 and 320x640 plus moved/zoomed views. Performance testing also exposed repeated vector submission at 2–4 frames/s; world-space caches preserve the artwork while achieving 52.5/56.4/57.5 frames/s in normal/moving/wide hardware-GPU samples. Excavated terrain and new buildings were inspected after cache invalidation. The earlier flat-map screenshots and failed performance/terrain evidence remain recoverable in ignored delivery evidence.
